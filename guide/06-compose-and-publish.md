# 6. Composition and publishing

The last mile: turning scenes, voice, captions and pictures into one file, in the right shape for the platform, with a cover that gets the click and a description that keeps the lawyers away. This chapter is short on theory and long on decisions.

## The cut follows the voice

In a faceless video the edit is mostly already made by the time the pictures arrive: the narration decides how long each scene lasts, and the pause between scenes is the cut. What remains is restraint.

- **Scene length is narration length.** A scene is on screen for as long as its words are spoken, plus the breath after them. Do not pad scenes to a fixed length and do not cut them short of their last word.
- **Transitions are nearly invisible.** A short fade between scenes is enough; it tells the eye that the picture changed on purpose. Wipes, spins and zoom-punches draw attention to the edit and away from the idea. Use a hard cut when the rhythm wants one.
- **Movement inside a scene is for still pictures.** A slow push or drift on a photograph keeps it from feeling frozen; video clips already move and need nothing added.
- **Cards are for chapters, not scenes.** A title card at the start, a chapter card where a long video changes subject, an end card. A card between every scene is a slideshow.

## Backgrounds and themes

Not every picture fills the frame, and some scenes have no picture at all (a quote, a number, a diagram). What shows behind them is the series' background, and it should be a decision, not a default.

A theme is a background and a palette that every scene can fall back to: Go Motion offers a few (a deep blue, a cinematic dark, a vintage paper, a clean light, a technical grid) and the choice colours the whole series. Pick the one that matches the footage you expect: light themes fight dark archival pictures, dark themes make bright stock footage glow. Then keep it; a channel's background is part of its identity, like its voice and its caption style.

## Landscape and portrait

Sixteen by nine is YouTube and the web; nine by sixteen is Shorts, Reels and TikTok. They are different videos, not the same video cropped, and the format is chosen before the script is written.

What changes when the frame turns:

- **The hook gets faster.** A vertical feed decides in the first two or three seconds. The first scene of a portrait video is one sentence, and it is the promise.
- **Captions get bigger and shorter.** Fewer words per phrase, often one, larger relative to the frame, placed in the middle band where the platform's own buttons do not cover them (chapter 4).
- **Pictures are cropped tighter.** A landscape clip in a portrait frame shows a vertical slice; the subject has to be in that slice. Prefer portrait footage and centred subjects.
- **The length is shorter.** Not because the platform forbids length, but because the viewer's thumb is faster than their patience. Thirty to sixty seconds is the native range; a longer vertical video needs a reason.

Go Motion fixes the format when a series is created, because the planner writes to it (shorter scenes, a one-line hook), the captions are sized to it and the picture search prefers it. Making a portrait version of a landscape video is a new series from the same script, not a crop.

## Thumbnails

On YouTube the thumbnail is the hook before the hook: it decides whether the video is opened at all. Two ways to make one, and a few rules that apply to both.

- **Upload a cover you made.** Fastest, fully yours, no generation. It is cropped to the series format and nothing else happens to it.
- **Generate one in a style you like.** Pick a few covers from channels whose look you admire. What is learned from them is their **look** (composition, colours, light, mood), not their content: titles and faces are never copied, and the background is painted for your topic without any text. Your title is a separate layer that you write, size and colour yourself and can change without repainting.

Rules for either path:

- **Readable at thumbnail size.** A cover is seen at the width of a thumb. Three or four words of title, heavy weight, strong contrast, one subject.
- **The title is not the hook sentence.** The thumbnail title and the video title should say different things that together make one promise; repeating the same words wastes one of them.
- **Consistent across the channel.** Same title position, same type, same palette; a viewer should recognise your covers in a row of strangers.

## Export

The export is where the saved version of the series becomes a file. Go Motion renders on its own servers at 1080p and thirty frames a second, in H.264 with AAC audio, which every platform accepts without re-encoding, and delivers the captions as SRT and VTT next to the MP4. Longer videos are rendered in parallel segments on several machines, so a long export does not take proportionally longer than a short one.

Export the saved version, not the one you are still editing; the preview in the editor is the truth, and what you see there is what the render draws. If a scene is still waiting for a changed line to be re-voiced, the export waits until the voice matches the words, so the file never contains a caption for a sentence the voice does not say.

## Publishing checklist

Before you upload:

1. **Title.** A promise, not a description; different words from the thumbnail title.
2. **Description.** One or two lines that restate the hook, then the credit lines from `CREDITS.txt` for every picture that requires one, then any sources the script cited.
3. **Subtitles.** If the burned-in captions are off, upload the SRT; if they are on, you may still upload it, since the platform indexes the text.
4. **Chapters.** For videos long enough to have them, the scene or chapter titles with their timestamps; they were written as concrete noun phrases in chapter 1, which is exactly what chapter markers want.
5. **Thumbnail.** The PNG at the platform's size; check it at thumb size on a phone.
6. **Format.** 16:9 for YouTube and the web, 9:16 for Shorts, Reels and TikTok, never one cropped from the other.
7. **A pinned comment or an end screen** that points to the next video, so the payoff has somewhere to go.

## Checklist

- Scene length is narration length; transitions are fades or cuts; cards are rare.
- One background theme per channel, chosen for the footage you actually use.
- The format was chosen first; a portrait version is a separate cut.
- The thumbnail reads at thumb size, says something the title does not, and matches the channel's covers.
- The exported file is the saved version, with captions as SRT and VTT alongside.
- The description carries the hook, the credit lines and the sources.
